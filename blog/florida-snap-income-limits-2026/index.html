Florida SNAP Income Limits 2026 — Complete Table by Household Size

Florida food stamp (SNAP) eligibility uses a gross income limit of 200% of the 2026 federal poverty level — $5,500 a month for a family of four — and a net limit of 100%. Maximum benefits rose on October 1, 2026 to $1,023 a month for four people.

📅 Last verified October 1, 2026 • Sources: USDA FNS, HHS 2026 Poverty Guidelines, Florida DCF • ⏱️ 5 min read

Quick answer: A Florida household can generally get SNAP if its gross monthly income is at or below $2,660 (1 person), $3,607 (2), $4,554 (3) or $5,500 (4), and net income after deductions is at or below 100% of the poverty level.

Each additional person adds about $947/month to the gross limit and $473 to the net limit. Gross and net limits are based on the 2026 HHS poverty guidelines ($15,960/year for one person + $5,680 per additional person); maximum benefits are the USDA FY2027 allotments for the 48 states and DC.

How your SNAP amount is calculated

SNAP expects households to spend about 30% of their net income on food. Your benefit is the maximum allotment for your household size minus 30% of your net income. Example: a family of 4 with $1,500 net monthly income would receive about $573 ($1,023 − $450).

Net income is gross income minus a 20% earned-income deduction, the standard deduction, dependent-care costs, medical costs over $35 for members 60+ or disabled, child support paid, and an excess-shelter deduction when rent and utilities exceed half of your remaining income. That is why documenting rent and utility bills can noticeably raise your benefit.

Key Facts About Florida SNAP 2026

What is the SNAP gross income limit in Florida for 2026?

Florida uses broad-based categorical eligibility, so most households qualify with gross income at or below 200% of the federal poverty level: $2,660/month for 1 person, $4,554 for 3 and $5,500 for a family of 4. The federal 130% limit does not apply to most Florida households.

What is the net income limit?

After deductions for housing, utilities, child care and medical costs (for members 60+ or disabled), net income must be at or below 100% of the poverty level — $2,750/month for a family of 4 in 2026.

What is the maximum SNAP benefit in Florida?

From October 1, 2026 (federal fiscal year 2027) the maximum monthly allotment is $306 for 1 person, $562 for 2, $808 for 3 and $1,023 for 4. Most households receive less, because 30% of net income is subtracted.

Who only has to meet the net income test?

Households with a member aged 60 or older or a member with a disability only need to meet the 100% net income limit, not the gross limit. Households where everyone receives SSI or TANF are categorically eligible.

When do Florida SNAP numbers change?

Two dates each year: maximum allotments and deductions change every October 1 with the USDA cost-of-living adjustment, and income limits follow the HHS poverty guidelines published each January (the 2026 guidelines were released January 15, 2026).
