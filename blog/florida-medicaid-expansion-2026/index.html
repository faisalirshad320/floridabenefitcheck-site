Florida Medicaid Expansion 2026: Where Things Stand

Florida is one of 10 states that have not expanded Medicaid under the Affordable Care Act. About 800,000+ Floridians are stuck in the coverage gap — earning too much for current Medicaid but too little for ACA marketplace subsidies. Here is what is happening in 2026.

What Is the Coverage Gap?

The ACA was designed so that anyone earning up to 138% of the Federal Poverty Level (FPL) would qualify for expanded Medicaid, while those earning 100% FPL and above could buy subsidized insurance on the marketplace. When the Supreme Court made Medicaid expansion optional in 2012, Florida chose not to expand — leaving a gap.

In Florida today, a non-disabled adult without children qualifies for Medicaid only if their income is below 26% FPL (about $346/month for an individual). Above that, they earn too much for Medicaid but if they earn less than 100% FPL ($1,330/month), they cannot get ACA subsidies either. They fall into the gap.

The 2026 Ballot Push

Florida Decides Healthcare, a coalition of healthcare advocacy groups, is gathering signatures for a constitutional amendment that would require the state to accept federal Medicaid expansion funds. To qualify for the November 2028 ballot, they need:

  • ~880,062 verified petition signatures from registered voters
  • Required signatures spread across at least 14 of Florida's 28 congressional districts
  • Florida Supreme Court approval of the ballot language

Expansion would cover adults aged 19–64 with incomes up to 138% FPL. For 2026 that means:

What to Do If You Are in the Gap Right Now

  1. Federally Qualified Health Centers (FQHCs): Sliding-scale care based on income. Find one at findahealthcenter.hrsa.gov.
  2. HealthCare.gov: If income is at least 100% FPL ($15,960/year for individual), you qualify for marketplace subsidies. Open enrollment: Nov 1 – Jan 15.
  3. Hospital Charity Care: All non-profit hospitals must offer financial assistance. Income up to 200–400% FPL can qualify for free or discounted care.
  4. Prescription Assistance: NeedyMeds.org and patient assistance programs from drug manufacturers can cut medication costs to nearly $0.
  5. Free clinics: Volunteer-staffed clinics in most counties. Search the Florida Association of Free & Charitable Clinics.
  6. Children: If you have children, they likely qualify for Florida KidCare (up to 200% FPL).

Frequently Asked Questions

Did Florida expand Medicaid in 2026?

As of June 2026, Florida has NOT expanded Medicaid under the ACA. The state remains one of 10 holdouts. A ballot initiative campaign is collecting signatures to put expansion on the November 2028 ballot, but it has not yet qualified.

How many Floridians are in the Medicaid coverage gap?

Roughly 800,000 to 1.1 million Floridians fall into the coverage gap — earning too much for current Medicaid (26% of FPL for adults) but too little to qualify for ACA marketplace subsidies (which start at 100% FPL).

If Florida expands Medicaid, what would change?

Medicaid would cover adults up to 138% of the Federal Poverty Level — approximately $20,783/year for an individual or $35,632 for a family of three (2026). The federal government would pay 90% of the cost.

How can I help push Medicaid expansion in Florida?

Watch Florida Decides Healthcare (floridadecideshealthcare.org), Florida Voices for Health, and SEIU Florida for petition signing events. The ballot initiative needs ~880,062 verified signatures across 14 congressional districts to qualify.

What can I do for healthcare right now if I am in the coverage gap?

Apply at HealthCare.gov (open enrollment Nov 1 - Jan 15), Federally Qualified Health Centers offer sliding-scale care, hospital charity care programs cover emergency costs, and prescription assistance programs help with medications. See our Coverage Gap Calculator for personalized options.
